The correction that matters most

Seven candidates are fused loci, not isoform switches

Two isoforms of one gene always share some exonic sequence. Sixteen candidate pairs share none — zero exonic bases, with their two sides 5.9–136 kb apart. Asking Ensembl which gene each side’s reference transcript belongs to settles it: the assembler merged two neighbouring genes into a single XLOC locus, so the measured dIF is the relative expression of two different genes.

Affects the bench plan

PLD6 was one of the five both-sides priority genes in docs/NEXT_SESSION.md. MREG and THAP2 were in the assayable 25. None of the three should be assayed as a switch.

The one that clears

NCOA7 shares no exons either, but both its transcripts are ENSG00000111912. It is a genuine internal-promoter isoform pair — and it is the interferon-inducible NCOA7-B already described in PMID 30478388, which is about as good a prior as a candidate can have.

Open question #4, closed

Class B is not NMD-target purification

The compositional-rescue class — genes whose total output falls while the baseline-dominant isoform holds — had one appealing explanation: the gene is shedding non-productive transcripts and concentrating its functional output. Testing it needed PTC status for non-switching isoforms, which lived only in the reduced objects at 5–21% coverage. Carrying PTC and the whole orfAnalysis slot across from the unfiltered exports raises that to 86–93%. Three readings, none supporting purification:

  • Minor isoforms are more often PTC+ than the dominant one — in Class B genes and in background genes, at odds ratios of 75–319. That is a property of being a minor isoform, not of Class B.
  • No enrichment between gene sets. Minor-isoform PTC rate in Class B vs background: 14.4% vs 20.8% in T_HT and 14.9% vs 20.3% in T_UT — depleted, if anything — and null in H_HT and U_UT. And T_HT/T_UT are the same six libraries re-annotated, so that is one observation, not two.
  • PTC does not predict which minor isoforms are shed. Null in all four datasets, p = 0.27–0.98.
Worth knowing about that last test

Written first against iso_lfc it gave a clean four-of-four result in the opposite direction, p = 0.002–0.07 — and would have been reported. Class B minor isoforms sit at 0.07–0.45 expression units, and the PTC+ ones are 1.5–4.1× less abundant than the PTC− ones, so the +1 pseudocount pins their fold-changes near zero by construction. Re-run on dIF/IF1 — a difference of ratios computed without a pseudocount — with baseline abundance in the model and gene as a fixed effect, it is null. The confound is written out as consequence_class_b_abundance_confound.csv.

Across all 63 candidate pairs

These are promoter switches, not splicing switches

Typing each pair directionally — how the candidate differs from the isoform it is replacing — the structural signature is lopsided. Fifty-four of 63 pairs have terminal exons that do not overlap at all, meaning a distinct promoter or polyA site. Internal splicing events are the minority.

That sharpens rather than settles the cap-selection question already logged for the experimentalist: without 5′-cap verification, incomplete assemblies produce exactly this signature. Twelve candidates are c-class (contained within another transcript) and differ from their partner only at the 5′ end — structurally indistinguishable from a truncated assembly. They are flagged per row rather than dropped.

What these profiles do and do not establish

  • UniProt features describe the canonical protein. A region absent from the canonical — a novel exon, an extended terminus, anything a PacBio-only transcript contributes — has no annotation to lose or gain and simply does not appear. No listed feature change is not evidence of a silent switch.
  • Event typing here is pairwise and directional, not IsoformSwitchAnalyzeR’s each-isoform-against-the-pre-RNA scheme. The two will not agree isoform-for-isoform, and are not meant to.
  • Coding consequence is a prediction from ORF calls, not evidence a protein is made. analyzeORF ran upstream; nothing has confirmed translation, and the orf_origin for these is Predicted.
  • The 12 c-class 5′-only candidates stay open until the cap-selection question is answered. That question is for the experimentalist, not for more analysis.
  • Everything here is T_UT, one arm, n = 3. These are candidate descriptions to prioritise a validation experiment, not results.
Suggested order

RBKS first — the largest effect among the both-sides genes, an unambiguous catalytic-site loss, an NMD-targeted rising isoform, and no isoform-level prior art to be scooped by. Then ATRN and IRAK3, both of which have a literature-predicted answer to check against. NCOA7 is the strongest single-sided candidate for the same reason.
